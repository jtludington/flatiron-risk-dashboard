Methodology

From Prototype to Production

How this prototype was produced
Step 1Define the Decision

Predict a composite material adverse outcome over 18 months using 33 point-in-time project indicators.

Step 2Create Demonstration History

Generate 1,500 synthetic snapshots with explicit relationships, missingness, noise, and leakage controls.

Step 3Train and Test

Train CatBoost on 1,200 projects and evaluate it on a separate 300-project holdout.

Step 4Apply Model to Projects

Score 12 fictional projects and calculate the leading local SHAP drivers for each prediction.

Step 5Visualize the Model

Publish the portfolio, explanations, and methodology as a dashboard

Process

  1. Define the Decision, Outcome, and Data Needs: Agree on who uses the score, the adverse outcomes and prediction horizon, and the project indicators worth collecting.
  2. Build Dated Historical Snapshots: Reconstruct what was known at each decision date and attach later outcomes.
  3. Implement a Data Collection Protocol: Define which data points will be collected, who is responsible, how they will be captured, how often they will be updated, and how completeness and accuracy will be checked.
  4. Develop and Independently Validate: Train CATBoost model and test calibration and stability.
  5. Pilot with Development Team: Run alongside existing reviews, capture disagreements, and measure decision value.
  6. Deploy with Monitoring: Establish ownership, overrides, approval thresholds, retraining triggers, and reporting.

Principal challenges

  1. Point-in-Time Reconstruction: Historical record keeping may be inconsistent or incomplete. Records must also reflect what was known at each prediction date to avoid inflated performance.
  2. Fragmented Definitions: Status and risk judgments vary across teams, markets, and stages.
  3. Model Development Timeline: Collecting sufficient reliable data, training the model, and validating its performance could take substantial time, delaying the dashboard rollout.
  4. Calibration and Drift: Markets, queue rules, financing, technology, and execution practices change.
